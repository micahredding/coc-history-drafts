# Week 4 — The Rise and Fall of the Springfield Presbytery (1803–1804)

[← All weeks](.)

**The Story:** Barton W Stone and minister friends are brought up on trial. They withdrew, but refused to un-church the men who were un-churching them. When their own movement started becoming a sect, they killed it. In the process, they ascribed all religious authority to the gathered body of believers.

**The Idea**: Our movement is built on institutional self-sacrifice, and the willingness to lay down every obstacle to unity and freedom. And to relentlessly embrace the priesthood of all believers.

## Ideas

1. **Exit without excommunication.** They withdrew from the synod's jurisdiction — but pointedly *not* from its communion, refusing to un-church their judges. The movement's founding moral gesture.
2. **Institutions are mortal by design.** "It is appointed for all delegated bodies once to die." When a structure obstructs unity, the faithful act is willing dissolution — and they did it while *succeeding*, not while failing.
3. **Conscience has a price tag.** Stone tore up his own salary paper in front of his congregations, having already freed his slaves — "I preferred honesty and a good conscience to all these things."
4. **Freedom requires forbearance.** "Pray more and dispute less" — and the scattering of the will's own signers shows what happens when it isn't practiced.
